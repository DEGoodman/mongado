## Intro

Most 90-day plans are generic checklists. The right first 90 days depend on the team you inherit. A stable, high-performing team needs you to avoid breaking things. A team recovering from a bad manager needs consistency before anything else. A startup may need decisions in your third week.

This framework gives the same three phases for every context and then adjusts what you do in each one.

## Definitions

**Phases:**

- **Days 1–30, Listen**: understand the team, the system, and the politics before changing anything
- **Days 31–60, Prove**: share what you learned, agree on priorities, deliver 1–2 quick wins
- **Days 61–90, Direct**: set strategic direction and team norms, using the trust you've built

**Contexts** (most teams are a mix; pick the dominant one):

- **Established**: larger company, stable team, many cross-team dependencies
- **Startup**: early stage, few processes, revenue or growth pressure
- **Recovering**: low trust, often after a toxic or absent leader
- **High-performing**: healthy team that's already shipping well

## Days 1–30: Listen

**Week 1**

- 1:1 with every team member. Goal: understand their work and what frustrates them.
- Attend existing meetings without changing them
- Meet key stakeholders: product, design, adjacent teams, your manager
- Read recent incident reviews, retros, and team metrics

**Weeks 2–4**

- Shadow people doing real work
- Review the codebase, architecture, and known tech debt
- Find where delivery gets stuck
- Map the unwritten rules: who actually makes decisions, and what gets rewarded

**By day 30, you should be able to answer:**

1. **Team health**: Real morale? Flight risks? What motivates each person?
2. **Technical state**: What's fragile? What's working?
3. **Delivery**: Where does work stall? What's the actual cycle time?
4. **Culture**: Which behaviors get rewarded? How are decisions really made?
5. **Expectations**: What does success look like to your manager, stakeholders, and team?

Check: people are giving you honest answers, not polished ones.

## Days 31–60: Prove

- **Play it back**: "Here's what I heard. What did I miss?" This shows you listened and catches what you got wrong.
- **Set priorities together**: lay out the tradeoffs and let the team weigh in. Don't dictate.
- **Deliver 1–2 quick wins**
- **Set your operating rhythm**: meeting cadence, how you communicate, how decisions get made

**A good quick win is:**

- **Uncontroversial**: the team already wanted it
- **Visible**: people notice the difference
- **Small**: done in 2–4 weeks
- **Enabling**: makes existing work easier, rather than changing what the work is

Examples: fix a painful CI or deploy step, cancel a useless meeting, write the missing doc everyone asks about, speed up PR review turnaround.

Check: the team has seen evidence that you understand their problems and can get things done.

## Days 61–90: Direct

- **Set direction**: propose bigger changes now that you have context and some trust
- **Write down team principles**: how we work, quality bar, behaviors we reward
- **Start performance conversations**: you now know enough to give useful feedback
- **Draft a 6–12 month thesis**: the few things that matter most, and what you're choosing not to do

Check: your manager, peers, and team trust you to operate independently.

## Adjusting for Context

| | Days 1–30 | Days 31–60 | Days 61–90 |
|-|-----------|------------|------------|
| **Established** | Extra time on politics and cross-team dependencies. Identify what *shouldn't* change. | Career conversations, peer relationships. Quick win: a cross-team friction point. | Initiative tied to company goals. Show cross-team collaboration. |
| **Startup** | Compress. You may need to make decisions by day 20. Focus on revenue and growth blockers. Be hands-on technically. | Put out the single biggest fire. Add only the process that stops churn. Quick win: visible technical delivery. | Measurable business impact. Build processes that will hold at 2x the team size. |
| **Recovering** | Shorter, more frequent check-ins. Acknowledge past problems out loud. Keep every small commitment. | Be predictable. Create good shared experiences. Quick win: fix a complaint that was ignored. | Visible improvement. Early skeptics start to come around. |
| **High-performing** | Listen longer. Ask "What would make your job easier?" | Remove obstacles, find growth opportunities. Quick win: amplify something that already works. | Expand the team's scope and influence. Shield them from distractions. |

## Pace: When to Speed Up or Slow Down

**Move faster when:**

- There's an active crisis affecting customers or revenue
- The team is explicitly asking for direction
- The previous leader left a vacuum and things are getting more chaotic
- You've spotted a critical risk nobody else has

**Slow down when:**

- You don't understand why something exists
- The team is resisting and you don't know why
- You're still getting surface-level answers
- Your quick wins aren't landing

## Pitfalls

- **Changing meetings in week 1.** You don't yet know which ones matter. Observe first.
- **Quick wins the team didn't ask for.** A win you picked yourself reads as an agenda. Pick from their complaints.
- **Skipping the playback.** Without "here's what I heard," people can't tell whether you listened.
- **Treating every context the same.** A startup cadence on a recovering team erodes trust. A recovering-team cadence at a startup wastes weeks.

## Measuring Success at Day 90

- **Team**: people raise real problems, not surface ones. At least one hard conversation went well.
- **Delivery**: you shipped at least one meaningful improvement, and stakeholders see you as reliable.
- **Relationships**: your manager lets you operate independently. Peers see you as a collaborator, not a rival.
- **Strategy**: you can state your priorities for the next 6–12 months and the tradeoffs behind them.

Three questions you should be able to answer clearly:

1. What's working that I need to protect?
2. What one fix would unlock the most value?
3. What does this team need from me specifically?

If you can't, keep listening.

## Conclusion

Listen for 30 days, earn trust with small visible wins, then set direction. How fast you move through each phase depends on the team you inherited.
